WHY WE REMEMBER

A moment in history. A message for today.

Reformation Day is observed on October 31 in remembrance of the Protestant Reformation and Martin Luther's Ninety-Five Theses, traditionally associated with Wittenberg in 1517.

The Reformation called Christians back to the authority of Scripture, the centrality of Christ, and the grace of God revealed in the Gospel.

WITTENBERG 1517
THE BEGINNING 95

The Ninety-Five Theses.

On October 31, 1517, Martin Luther's Ninety-Five Theses were published in Wittenberg, Germany. The document questioned the practice of selling indulgences and called for serious theological discussion.

What followed became one of the most influential movements in Western Christian history.

REMEMBERING THE MOMENT

History in images.

The story of the Reformation has been remembered through generations of paintings, churches, writings, and testimonies.

WITTENBERG, GERMANY Castle Church
A PLACE REMEMBERED

The church door that became a symbol.

The Castle Church in Wittenberg became closely associated with the story of Luther and the Ninety-Five Theses.

The church stands today as a powerful reminder of the historical movement that began in sixteenth-century Europe.

THE HEART OF THE REFORMATION

Five truths. One Gospel.

The Five Solas summarize central Reformation convictions concerning Scripture, grace, faith, Christ, and the glory of God.

I Sola Scriptura Scripture Alone

God's Word as the supreme authority for Christian faith and life.

II Sola Gratia Grace Alone

Salvation is received by God's grace, not earned by human merit.

III Sola Fide Faith Alone

We receive God's saving grace through faith.

IV Solus Christus Christ Alone

Jesus Christ is at the center of salvation and the Gospel.

V Soli Deo Gloria Glory to God Alone

All glory belongs to God.
